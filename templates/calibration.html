This wizard computes the calibration offset — the constant difference between your microphone's raw digital reading (dBFS) and the actual sound pressure level (dB SPL) at the mic. Once computed, all dB readings on the dashboard and in incident logs reflect real-world SPL.

Step 1: Get a reference sound level

You need a known SPL value from any of these methods:

  • SPL meter — Hold a calibrated meter (or phone app like NIOSH SLM) next to your Noise Warden mic. Play a steady tone or pink noise. Read the SPL (e.g., 72.0 dB).
  • Calibrator — Acoustic calibrators output a precise 94 or 114 dB tone directly into the mic capsule. Use the printed value.
  • Known source — A vacuum at 3 ft is roughly 70–75 dB. Less precise, but workable for an initial rough calibration.

Enter that number in Reference SPL below.

Step 2: Capture the raw dBFS

While the reference sound is playing, click the Raw dBFS pill at the top of this page. It will auto-fill the Observed raw dBFS field below with the current reading.

Step 3: Compute and save

Give this profile a name (e.g., "Living room BY-MM1") so you can track different mic/placement combos over time.

Noise Floor Gate

  

    The ambient noise floor sets the minimum dBA level worth analyzing. Signals below this
    threshold are treated as background white noise and skip the DSP pipeline entirely
    (spectrum analysis, beat detection, classification filters). This reduces CPU usage on
    the Pi and avoids processing blocks that could never trigger an incident.
